#### Background
- Low fertility and population aging are reshaping the population structure, and as industries move to cities, local young populations are shrinking.
- Aging houses are concentrated in poor housing environments such as hillside areas and semi-basements, and the number of vacant houses grows every year.
- Vacant houses lead to blighted landscapes, safety problems, and population decline, and **the problem is more severe in more aged areas**.

#### Research Questions
- What do cases that revived abandoned vacant houses as local assets have in common?
- How do appropriate technology and collaboration among diverse actors contribute to the sustainability of vacant house reuse?

#### Cases & Methods
- I reviewed 9 prior studies on the concepts of vacant houses, appropriate technology, and ESG, and on the use of idle spaces, and reinterpreted them from an ESG perspective.
- I examined 6 domestic and international cases: Dazayo in Jeju, Seogyo Art Experiment Center, the Onomichi Vacant House Renewal Project, the Yanaka historic district in Taito City, the Westway Trust in the UK, and the Incheon Smart Urban Farmer Cooperative.
- For each case, I compared the operating model (Type), Actor, Activity, Resource, and Sustainability.

#### Key Findings
- Dazayo in Jeju leases vacant houses free of charge for 10 years, renovates them into lodging, recovers costs through rental income, and then returns them to the original owners. Part of the revenue goes to a village development fund.
- In Onomichi and Yanaka, resident-led NPOs collected information on vacant houses and converted them into cafés, guesthouses, and share houses.
- The Westway Trust transferred operation from the local government to a social enterprise and worked with about 60 resident groups to run the space under an elevated highway as a community sports and gathering space.
- The Smart Urban Farmer Cooperative grows mushrooms in vacant semi-basement homes, creating jobs for vulnerable groups such as seniors and women with career breaks.
- The lasting cases shared **resident participation, public-private governance, and revenue structures that do not depend solely on public support**.

<p class="figure-note">The original report compared 6 cases by Type, Actor, Activity, Resource, and Sustainability; here I selected the 4 with clearly different operating models. Even when the public sector provides the space, cases tend to last longer as operation and revenue shift to residents and the private sector.</p>

#### Implications
- Environment: Vacant houses should be seen as assets that hold local history and culture, and appropriate technology should be used to find uses suited to local conditions.
- Social: Residents should participate directly, and opportunities for community participation and jobs should be expanded for vulnerable groups.
- Governance: Long-term operation requires **a clear operating entity** and a system for coordinating stakeholder views.

#### Limitations & Next Steps
- Because I redefined ESG at the spatial level as "a strategy for revitalizing the local economy through environmental conservation and stronger social solidarity," direct comparison with existing ESG research was difficult.
- The scope of the case research and legal constraints also remain limitations.
